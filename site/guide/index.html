1The main views

HOME flips between the main views.
RACK shows the channels of the current page.
CHIP allows you to edit the patch of the chip being used in the selected channel.
SONG is the order your pages play in.
The top bar shows the console of the current channel, the page you're on, the note under the cursor and the tempo.
Hold HOME to open the project view.
GLO shows the help for whatever view you're on.
GLO + HOME opens the menu. Its first line opens the lessons: turn SELECT to pick one and press OCT+. A tick marks the ones you finished. A card over the bottom of the screen says what to do next and goes green when you have done it. GLO hides the card and brings it back. The lesson works on a scratch project and your own comes back when it ends. The menu has four groups: Lessons, Settings (Quick Start Always, Projects At Start, Show Tips, Dancing Animations, Show Battery), Sync and Link (MIDI Clock, Brothers Link) and Reset (Restore Demos, Factory Reset). OCT+ opens a group, OCT- goes back.
SEQ opens step entry for a channel (or the drum lanes).
FX is used for punch-in effects.
HOME will always 'go back' when you're in a submenu.

2Lessons

The lessons walk you through the whole workflow, one instruction at a time. GLO + HOME opens the menu; its first line is Lessons, a list with a tick on the ones you finished. Turn SELECT to pick one and press OCT+.

A card on the screen names the next thing to do and the control to use. It goes green when you have done it, then the next step shows.
GLO puts the card away and brings it back. Your own project is set aside while a lesson runs and comes back when it ends. Nothing a lesson does is saved.
On the last card, OCT+ starts the next lesson and OCT- ends this one.
There is a lesson for every chip. The four masterclasses, in gold in the list, go deep into why a sound behaves as it does: FM, the SID, wave tables and drums. Pages to read (a window: OCT+ goes on, HOME puts it away, GLO brings it back) alternate with things to do on the screen.

3Channels

A channel is one voice of one chip.
You can have up to 16 channels, in any mix.
Select the + row at the bottom of the rack with PRESETS, then press OCT+ to add a channel.
Choose a console, then a channel. OCT- goes back a step.
Play the keys and the console on the bar answers, so you can hear it before you add it.
KNOB1 groups the consoles by type, year, maker or country.
Leave a console for five seconds and notes about it scroll up the bottom of the screen.
SEL opens a card for the console: a bigger picture, its year and maker. SELECT turns to the next one.
PRESETS selects the channel in the RACK, CHIP and STEP views.
Hold EDIT on a channel to remove it.
Hold SEL on a channel to copy it. You can copy the patch only, or the patch and the notes.
Tap EDIT on a channel to give it another console. Its notes stay. Going between notes and drums clears the track, so you are asked first: OCT+ for yes, OCT- for no.
Turn KNOB 2 to set the channel's level. A bar under its name shows it for a moment.
Turn KNOB 3 to slide the channel's steps along the page.
Turn KNOB 4 to duck the channel when a kick plays. Any drum channel's kick counts.

4The rack

Each row is a channel.
A page is 16 steps, which is one bar.
KNOB 1 sets the tempo, from 40 to 240 bpm.
ALGORITHM flips through the pages.
PLAY plays the song from the current page.
Hold PLAY to loop just the current page.
SELECT is the tape dial. Turn it right to play (half speed, normal speed or double speed) or left to play backwards. The middle position pauses.
While the tape is stopped, SELECT moves the cursor.
While the tape is stopped, a key writes its note on the cursor's step. Press the same key again to remove it.
OCT- and OCT+ move the keys down or up an octave, by up to two octaves.
ARP shows or hides note tails, the line under a long note.

5Writing notes

There are three ways to write notes. You can mix them.

Step entry

SEQ opens the current page as blocks, 16 to a screen.
A key sets the block under the cursor and moves to the next one.
SELECT moves the cursor, so you can leave rests.
EDIT clears the block.
KNOB 2 sets the length of a note, in steps. Turn it while you hold a key, or while the cursor is on a note.
KNOB 3 sets the meter of the page and KNOB 4 sets its length in bars. KNOB 1 sets the tempo of this page alone, and SEL glides it into the next page's. See Meter and tempo.
PLAY loops the page while you work.

Recording

REC loops the page and starts recording.
A key lands on the step nearest the playhead.
Hold a key to set the length of the note.
Hold EDIT to erase notes as the playhead passes them.
Turn SELECT left once to record at half speed.
Drums can't be recorded live. Use the drum lanes.

6The chip screen

CHIP shows the patch of the selected channel.
KNOB 1 to KNOB 4 change the four cards on screen.
ALGORITHM flips between the pages of cards.

Page 1: the tone

CardWhat it does
DUTY, WAVE, BUZZ, NOISE or ALGOThe first knob. It depends on the chip. See the consoles below.
VOLSets the level of the channel.
VIBSets the depth of the vibrato.
SWEEPSlides the pitch up or down while a note is held. On the NES pulse, far from the middle it moves several notes a step.
PANSets left, centre or right. Chips with stereo have PAN instead of SWEEP, and keep SWEEP on a page of its own where the real chip has one.

Page 2: the envelope

ATK is how long the sound takes to rise.
DEC is how long it takes to fall to SUS.
SUS is the level it holds while the key is down.
REL is how long it takes to fade out after you let go.

Page 3: the arp

SHAPE picks the chord.
The chords are octave, major, minor, major 7th, minor 7th, sus4, power and diminished.
RATE sets the speed.
DIR is up, down, or up and down.
VSPD sets how fast the vibrato goes.

Page 4: waves and FM

Channels with a wave have a fourth page: SHAPE, FOLD, STEP and TWIST.
SHAPE morphs between pulse, saw, triangle and sine.
FOLD folds the wave back on itself, which makes it brighter.
STEP reduces the number of levels, which crushes the wave.
TWIST bends the wave sideways.

Draw your own wave

The Game Boy, PC Engine, Virtual Boy, WonderSwan, Konami SCC, Namco 163, Famicom Disk and Namco System 1 waves have a last page, DRAW. Turn ALGORITHM to reach it.
Turn SELECT to pick a step of the wave. Turn KNOB 1 to set its height.
Turn KNOB 2 to move 1, 2, 4 or 8 steps at once. Turn KNOB 3 to copy your edits to the far side of the wave.
The wave is stored at the chip's own depth: 4 bits on the Game Boy, 5 on the PC Engine, 6 on the Virtual Boy and Famicom Disk, 8 on the SCC.
Turn a SHAPE, FOLD, STEP or TWIST knob to go back to the generated wave. LFO undoes it.
FM chips have operators instead: see FM chips below.

7The consoles

ConsoleNotes
NESPulse has four duties: 12.5, 25, 50 and 75 percent. Triangle has no volume knob. Noise has MODE, the long or the short shift register, and PERIOD: 0 follows the note, 1 to 15 fix the rate. The output goes through the NES's own filters.
Game BoyPulse has four duties. Wave has four volume steps and stereo. Noise has WIDTH, the long or the short register, and stereo; each note picks the nearest of the chip's noise rates. Pulse and wave have a SWEEP page. The output goes through the Game Boy's high-pass filter.
Master SystemTone is a square wave with 16 volume levels. NOISE picks tone, white or periodic noise that follows the note, or the chip's three fixed noise rates of each.
PC EngineWave has 16 volume levels and stereo. Noise has RATE: 0 follows the note, 1 to 31 fix it. Wave has a SWEEP page.
Atari 2600TYPE picks one of eleven sound types, from clean tones to buzzes to white noise.
Konami VRC6Pulse has eight duties. Saw is a stepped saw wave, twice as loud as the pulse at the same volume. OVER raises its rate past the usual top, so the saw wraps and turns rough.
AY-3-8910Tone is a square wave with 16 volume levels. NOISE 0 is tone only. 1 to 14 gate the tone with noise, a faster noise the higher you go. 15 is noise alone, which is useful for hats and snares. A fourth page has PERIOD, which fixes the noise rate, and BUZZ, which plays the chip's envelope as the sound, with BRATIO setting its speed against the note.
Mega DriveFM has four operators and eight algorithms, through the chip's grainy 9 bit output. See FM chips.
Virtual BoyWave has 32 steps of 6 bits, which is finer than the Game Boy. It has stereo. Noise has TAP, which picks the shift register's feedback tap. Wave has a SWEEP page. Notes below about D2 play at the chip's lowest pitch.
Atari 8-bitDIST picks one of eight distortions, from a clean tone through buzzes to noise. A fourth page has HPF, the high-pass filter, and CLOCK, which runs the noise from the 15 kHz or the 1.79 MHz clock.
PC SpeakerOne square wave with no volume. Notes are a little out of tune, like the real timer. The sound goes through a small speaker's filter.
AdLibFM has two operators. See FM chips.
Namco WSGWave has 32 steps of 4 bits and 15 volume levels. The output goes through a low-pass filter.
Namco 163Wave works like the WSG, and LENGTH sets how many steps the wave has, from 4 to 64. The chip shares one converter between its voices, so more channels playing at once sound grittier and quieter.
Sunsoft 5BTone and NOISE work like the AY-3-8910, with 3 dB volume steps. BUZZ runs the envelope as the sound: off, falling, rising, triangle or a pluck. RATE sets its speed against the note, and MIXENV plays the tone together with it.
NES MMC5Pulse works like the NES pulse: four duties and the NES's own mixer. There is no triangle and no sweep.
Famicom DiskWave has 32 steps of 6 bits and goes through a low-pass filter. MOD sets how hard the chip's modulator bends the wave, from off to deep.
Konami SCCWave has 32 steps of 8 bits. Like all the chips with a divider, high notes land on the chip's steps and can be a little out of tune.
MSX MusicINST picks one of the chip's 15 built-in instruments, or your own. See FM chips.
Konami VRC7Works like MSX Music, with the VRC7's own 15 instruments. See FM chips.
Arcade FMFM has four operators and a LFO. ALGO picks one of eight ways to connect them. It has stereo. See FM chips.
Sound BlasterFM has four operators. CONN picks one of four connections, and every operator has its own wave. It has stereo. See FM chips.
WonderSwanWave has 32 steps of 4 bits and stereo. The chip's output is sampled at 24 kHz, so it sounds crunchier than the Namco chips. Notes below about G1 play at the chip's lowest pitch.
Sam CoupeTone and NOISE work like the AY-3-8910, with linear volume and stereo. While the noise is high the tone is half as loud. A fourth page has the envelope generator: ENV picks a shape, ERES makes the steps coarser, NRATE fixes the noise rate.
VIC-20The pitch is a 7 bit divide, so high notes are a little out of tune, like the real chip. TYPE 1 swaps the square wave for the noise voice.
Plus/4TYPE 1 swaps the square wave for noise. Notes below A2 play an octave higher, because the chip can't go lower.
Atari LynxTYPE 1 is a square wave. The rest run the chip's shift register with different taps: short ones sound like buzzy tones, long ones like noise. It has stereo. A fourth page has INTEG, which integrates the output, TAPS, which picks another short cycle, and BAL, a finer balance than PAN.
PC-98FM works like Arcade FM, with stereo. SSG works like the AY-3-8910.
Neo GeoThe same as PC-98: FM with stereo, and an SSG like the AY-3-8910.
Neo Geo PocketTone is a square wave with stereo. It works like the Master System's, noise included. It has a SWEEP page.
MSX-AudioFM has two operators, A and C. ALGO picks FM or both playing. It has no wave choices, unlike the AdLib.
Yamaha TX81ZFM has four operators and the eight Arcade FM algorithms. Each operator has its own wave, one of eight: sine, sine squared, half sine, half sine squared, alternating sine, alternating sine squared, camel sine and camel sine squared.
SN76477MIX picks tone, tone with noise, noise, or siren. SLF swings the pitch up and down, from off to fast. The siren swings it twice as far. A fourth page has FILTER, RATE, DEPTH and DUTY for the noise filter, the noise rate, how deep the slow oscillator swings and the square's width.
Namco System 1Wave works like the WSG, with stereo.
Korg Poly-800An organ. FOOT turns four pitches on or off: 16' (an octave down), 8' (the note), 4' and 2' (one and two octaves up). QUINT adds a fifth above the note, or a fifth above the octave, or both.
ZX SpectrumA pulse wave with no volume. DUTY sets how thin the pulse is. The speaker is one bit, so every voice shares it and chords crunch.
NEC PC-88FM works like Arcade FM, with one speaker. SSG works like the AY-3-8910.
Commodore 64One voice of the SID, with its own filter. It has five pages of knobs. See below.

FM chips

Press SEL on an FM chip's screen to pick what the knobs edit: the channel, then each operator in turn. The picture lights the operator you are on.
Turn ALGORITHM for the pages of the channel or the operator.
Every operator has the chip's own settings. Nothing is shared: each operator has its own level, multiple, envelope and, where the chip has them, wave and flags.
TL is the operator's level. Higher is quieter. On a modulator it sets how hard the operator pushes the one it feeds, so lower TL makes a brighter sound.
MUL sets how many times the note's pitch the operator runs at. DT1 detunes it a little. DT2 on Arcade FM and the TX81Z lifts it by a fixed interval.
AR, D1R, D1L, D2R and RR are the envelope: attack rate, first fall, the level where the first fall ends, a slow fall after it, and the release. A higher rate is faster. D1L is a level: higher is quieter.
KS makes high notes run their envelopes faster. AM lets the LFO's tremolo shake the operator's level.
The channel's pages have the algorithm, FB (the feedback of operator 1), PAN, VOL, and the LFO.
Arcade FM and the TX81Z LFO has a speed, a wave, and two depths: PMD for vibrato, AMD for tremolo. PMS and AMS pick how much of each reaches the channel. The Mega Drive, PC-98 and Neo Geo LFO has eight speeds.
The PC-88 chip has no LFO and no panning.
The TX81Z also has FINE steps between multiples, a SHIFT that narrows an envelope, and FIX, which plays a fixed pitch whatever the note. WAVE picks one of eight shapes for each operator.

Algorithms of Arcade FM, the Mega Drive and the others

ALGO 1: 1, 2, 3 and 4 in a chain. Only 4 is heard.
ALGO 2: 1 and 2 both modulate 3, which modulates 4. Only 4 is heard.
ALGO 3: 2 modulates 3. 1 and 3 both modulate 4. Only 4 is heard.
ALGO 4: 1 modulates 2. 2 and 3 both modulate 4. Only 4 is heard.
ALGO 5: 1 modulates 2, and 3 modulates 4. 2 and 4 are heard.
ALGO 6: 1 modulates 2, 3 and 4. All three are heard.
ALGO 7: 1 modulates 2. 2, 3 and 4 are heard.
ALGO 8: all four play on their own.
The Mega Drive's output goes through the chip's 9 bit converter, which adds a grainy edge to quiet notes.

AdLib, MSX-Audio and Sound Blaster

AdLib and MSX-Audio have two operators. Operator 1 modulates operator 2. CNT switches to both playing on their own.
TL, MULT, KSL (key scale level, quieter high notes) and WAVE are on the first page. AR, DR, SL and RR are on the second.
The third page has TREM and VIB, which switch the chip's tremolo and vibrato on for the operator, HOLD, which keeps the level until you let go, and KSR, which makes high notes run their envelopes faster.
TREMD and VIBD on the channel make the tremolo and vibrato deeper.
The AdLib has four waves for each operator. MSX-Audio has only the sine.
The Sound Blaster has four operators, eight waves for each one, and four connections. CONN 1: 1, 2, 3 and 4 in a chain. CONN 2: 1 plays on its own, and 2 modulates 3, which modulates 4. CONN 3: 1 modulates 2, and 3 modulates 4. CONN 4: 1 and 4 play on their own, and 2 modulates 3.

MSX Music and Konami VRC7

INST picks one of 15 built-in instruments. The instrument's settings are copied into the operators, so you can change any of them. A mark by INST shows that you did. INST 0 keeps what you have.
INST 1 to 15 for MSX Music: Violin, Guitar, Piano, Flute, Clarinet, Oboe, Trumpet, Organ, Horn, Synth, Harpsichord, Vibraphone, Synth Bass, Acoustic Bass, Electric Guitar.
INST 1 to 15 for the VRC7: Buzzy Bell, Guitar, Wurly, Flute, Clarinet, Synth, Trumpet, Organ, Bells, Vibes, Vibraphone, Tutti Frutti, Fretless, Synth Bass, Sweep.
The modulator has TL, KSL, MULT and WAVE (sine or half sine). The carrier has no level of its own: VOL sets it. PEDAL makes the release slower.

Commodore 64 voice

WAVE picks triangle, saw, saw with triangle, pulse, pulse with triangle, pulse with saw, all three, or noise. The combined waves come from real chips. Some of them are silent at some pulse widths, like the real ones.
CHIP picks a 6581 or an 8580. They differ in the combined waves and the filter.
ATK, DEC, SUS and REL are the chip's own envelope, with the same times.
Page 4: PW is the width of the pulse. PWM sweeps it back and forth, faster the higher you turn it.
SYNC makes a hidden second oscillator restart the voice. The higher the setting, the higher the voice sounds.
RING ring-modulates the voice with a hidden oscillator. Triangle sounds best.
Page 5: CUT and RES set the filter. MODE lights L, B and H for low, band and high pass, in any mix. FENV opens the cutoff with the envelope.

8Drums

Every console has its own drum kit.
The drums are BD (kick), SD (snare), LT, MT and HT (toms), RS (rimshot), CP (clap), CH (closed hat), OH (open hat), CR (crash) and RD (ride).
SEQ opens the drum lanes, with one row per drum and 16 steps across.
Use the black keys to change the drum in the kit.
The 16 white keys align to the 16 steps in the page.
Hold ENV while you press a white key to place an accent, a louder hit. On a hit that is already there, ENV switches the accent on or off.
EDIT clears the picked drum on the current page.

9The song

SONG shows a row of slots. Each slot holds a page.
A page can be used in more than one slot.
SELECT moves the cursor.
Use the PRESETS knob to change the page used in the selected slot.
OCT+ adds a slot after the current one, with a new empty page.
OCT- removes the slot.
Tap EDIT to open the slot's page in the RACK.
Hold EDIT to copy the page. The copy replaces the page in this slot only.
PLAY plays the song from the current slot.

10Meter and tempo

Every page has a meter, a length and, if you want one, a tempo of its own. A new page starts in 4/4, one bar long, at the song's tempo.

Meter and length

Open a page with SEQ and turn KNOB 3 to pick the meter: 2/4, 3/4, 4/4, 5/4, 6/8 or 7/8.
The page is as long as its meter says: 16 steps in 4/4, 12 in 3/4 and 6/8, 14 in 7/8, 20 in 5/4. Steps past the end are crossed out and skipped.
A note on a crossed-out step stays, greyed out. Make the page longer and it returns.
The stripes on the steps show where each group of beats starts. The dots under the tempo count them while it plays.
A new page starts with the meter of the page before it. A copy keeps the meter of the original.

Long pages

Turn KNOB 4 to add bars. A page can have up to 64 steps: four bars in 4/4, five in 3/4.
A page of more than 16 steps shows 16 at a time. The line under the top bar has a piece for each screen, and the one you are on is lit.
In step entry, SELECT moves the cursor onto the next screen. On a drum channel, SELECT turns whole screens.
In the RACK the screen follows the playhead while the page plays.
Change the length of a page while it plays and the new length starts the next time round.

A tempo for a page

In step entry, turn KNOB 1. The page gets a tempo of its own and the tempo turns amber.
Turn it back to the song's tempo and the page lets go.
Outside step entry, KNOB 1 changes the tempo you see: the page's own if it has one, the song's if it doesn't.
A page without a tempo of its own plays at the song's. A new page starts with the tempo of the page before it.
When you follow an outside MIDI clock or a Brothers Link leader, the page tempos are ignored. Their clock rules.

Glide

Tap SEL in step entry. The page glides from its tempo into the tempo of the next page in the song. A little curve by the tempo shows how, going down for a slower page and up for a faster one.
Tap SEL again for the next shape. Straight moves at a steady pace. Ease in waits and changes late, which is how a ritardando digs in at the end. Ease out changes early and settles. One more tap and the glide is off.
The tempo moves a little at every step, so the number counts while it plays.
A page in the last slot has nowhere to glide to, and a page you loop never glides.
Want a ritardando at the end? Glide the last page into a slower one after it.

Try it

Projects has a sample called Meters waltz: an original waltz that goes through 3/4, 6/8, 5/4, 7/8, 4/4 and 2/4, with a 64 step page and two glides. Test: meters and a waltz is a plain version for trying the controls.

11Projects

Hold HOME to open the project view. It has twelve tiles, each with a small picture of the first page.
SELECT or PRESETS moves between tiles. OCT+ opens a tile. OCT- goes back.
The first two tiles are the demos. They don't save changes, so copy one before you edit it.
Hold EDIT on a demo to delete it. Its slot becomes an ordinary one (tiles 11 and 12). Restore Demos in the menu (GLO + HOME) brings the demo back to an empty slot.
Open an empty tile to start a new project.
Hold SEL on a tile to copy that project into the first empty tile.
Hold EDIT on a tile to delete that project.
Projects save automatically when nothing is playing. Hit SAVE to force a save just in case.
The SAVE light glows while there are unsaved changes.
Factory Reset in the menu (GLO + HOME) deletes all your projects and brings the demos back.

12Undo

At any time, you can undo changes with the LFO key.
NOTE: you can't redo an undone change, so be careful!

15Bluetooth sync

Chipbox, Smileyface and Drumlab can play together over Bluetooth. One leads, the others follow. No cables, no pairing.
Hit GLO + HOME and pick Bluetooth. On the leader, set Sync to Lead. On the others, set it to Follow. OCT+ changes it.
The status line says Searching while it looks for a leader, Linked when it hears one, and In sync once it is playing along.
Hit PLAY on the leader. The followers start at the next bar and take its tempo. Stop the leader and they stop.
Sidechain: the leader tells the followers where its kicks are. The followers duck on every one, so the notes pump against the beat. Chipbox ducks its notes and leaves its own drums alone.
Patterns: when the leader changes pattern, a following Chipbox moves to that page when the one it plays ends. A page with nothing in it is skipped. It plays the page round and round. The leader's swing comes with it where there is a swing to follow.
Punch-in FX: hold one of pads 1 to 8 on the leader and every follower does it too: TAPE, RPT, LPF, HPF, ECHO, VERB, CRSH and GATE. Pads 9 to 15 are different on each FM-1 and don't travel.
Key: Chipbox works out the key of what it plays and tells the others. A following Chipbox moves its notes to the nearest note of the leader's key, so nothing clashes. This one only does something when the leader has notes. A leading Drumlab has none.
Demo loads a locked project to play along with the other two: D minor, 124 BPM, two pages. Nothing in it is saved. Open any project to leave it.
Any brother can lead, but only one at a time. The switches are remembered when you turn it off and on again. Sync is always Off after power-up.
Before you install a new firmware, set Sync to Off. While the radio runs, the FM-1 can stop answering the installer.

16MIDI keyboard

Plug a keyboard's 5-pin MIDI out into the FM-1's 3.5 mm midi. Use an adaptor. Get a good one lmao, break a $10 note to get it haha.
Have fun and experiment! I'm planning on making a way to map the controls, but for the most part controls like this work: PLAY, STOP, REC, KNOBS, SLIDERS, the PITCH and MOD wheels. OH! and the actual keys!
